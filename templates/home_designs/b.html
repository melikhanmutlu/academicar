Abstract

Upload a GLB, STL, OBJ, FBX or STEP file, or a CT/MR scan, and AcademicAR returns a browser-based 3D viewer, a mobile AR view and a stable QR code. Readers need no app and no account.

1Introduction

Most three-dimensional research still reaches its readers as a flat screenshot. A segmented heart, a fossil scan or a gearbox assembly is rotated once in specialist software, captured at a single angle and pasted into a PDF. Depth, scale and the relationship between parts are left for the reader to imagine.1

When the original files are shared at all, they sit in a supplementary archive that needs the right application to open, and the link tends to break when a repository moves. We built AcademicAR so the model itself can travel with the work: one link, one QR code, opened in any modern browser.

1Static figures show one angle. Readers cannot check a structure from behind, cut through it or measure it.

2Methods

2.1Upload

Add a model to a project and confirm that it is anonymised, that you hold the rights and that ethics responsibilities are met. Medical scans ask for one more confirmation, and the raw scan is deleted once conversion finishes.2

2.2Conversion

A background worker converts every input to an optimised GLB with compressed geometry, builds a USDZ companion for iPhone AR, and keeps multi-part models as named layers. CT and MR series are thresholded with presets or your own Hounsfield range.

2.3Stable link and QR code

Each model gets a short resolver link and a QR code. Replace the file, rescale it or change its colours and the printed code keeps pointing at the current version.

2.4Sharing

Keep a model private while you work, send a review link to co-authors or examiners, or make it public when the paper is out.

3Results

The viewer gives readers the tools they would otherwise need desktop software for. Every tool runs in the browser, and owners can save what a reader should see first as a named scene.

4Availability

Readers
Open the viewer from a link or a QR code on a poster, slide or thesis page. Augmented reality works on recent iPhones, iPads and Android phones without an app, through iOS Quick Look and WebXR.
Websites
Embed the viewer on a lab page, course site or online article. How embedding works
Institutions
Universities and departments fund their members' models from a shared quota, invite people by link, manage them from an institution panel and get a public showcase page. See the institution dashboard
Licensing
Plans are per model, so each model carries its own access period and storage. Start on the free plan and upgrade only the models that need it. Compare plans
